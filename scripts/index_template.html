What Makes a Good Layer?

Assessing the Layer-Wise Intrinsic Properties of Music Foundation Models

Angelos-Nikolaos Kanatas1, Yuexuan Kong2,3, Pablo Alonso-Jiménez1, Xavier Serra1, Dmitry Bogdanov1. ISMIR 2026.

1Music Technology Group, Universitat Pompeu Fabra; 2Deezer Research; 3Nantes Université, École Centrale Nantes, CNRS, LS2N

Layer-wise downstream performance of {{NMODELS}} music foundation models on {{NTASKS}} tasks. The label-free metrics of each layer and their correlation with performance are on the Metrics page.

Each cell reports the best layer of a model for a task, its score and the profile across layers. Select a cell to open the full curve, or a column header to sort. Headers marked ▸ expand to further metrics of the same datasets. Params counts the evaluated encoder (the audio tower of two-tower models, the decoder of autoregressive models).

For each model and task family, the three layers with the highest mean score after min-max normalization of each task's layer profile, with the best of the three, its relative depth and the mean profile. Hover over a cell for the best layer of each task.

Each layer of the frozen encoder is evaluated separately with a trained probe, an MLP with one hidden layer of 512 units or a linear probe (we will extend this to further probe architectures, such as attentive probes). Scores may differ from those reported in the original papers, which use other probes, checkpoints or splits. Per-layer results are available in the data folder.
